# Template: Standard Consulting Services Proposal

**Use when:** any advisory/consulting services opportunity (the firm's default proposal template unless a client or opportunity calls for a different structure).

**Defined by:** Chris York (board), 2026-07-11, issue NEW-8.

## Tone
Confident, plain-spoken, client-outcome-first. Short paragraphs. No buzzword chains.

## Sections, in order

1. **Executive Summary** — 3-5 sentences: client's situation, what we propose, the outcome, why us. (Standing board rule: every client-facing document opens with this regardless of template.)
2. **Understanding of Your Needs** — restate the client's challenge in their own terms; demonstrate we listened before proposing anything.
3. **Proposed Approach & Methodology** — phased approach under a named methodology; describe what happens in each phase.
4. **Scope of Services** — bulleted list of in-scope services; be explicit about what is included (pairs with Assumptions & Exclusions for what is NOT included).
5. **Timeline & Milestones** — table of phases, durations, and key milestones.
6. **Team & Qualifications** — named roles (not necessarily named individuals) and relevant experience for each.
7. **Investment** — pricing table or range, plus payment terms. Never invent numbers — pull from board-supplied figures only, mark [TBD: pricing] if not yet provided.
8. **Assumptions & Exclusions** — what we assume the client will provide/do; what is explicitly out of scope.
9. **Why Us** — 3-4 differentiators tied specifically to this client's situation (not generic firm boilerplate).
10. **Acceptance** — signature block and proposal validity period.

## Required placeholders / facts to gather during interview
- Client name and organization
- Client's stated challenge/situation (in their words, for section 2)
- Named methodology and phase breakdown
- Scope items (in and out)
- Timeline: phase durations and milestone dates
- Team roles and relevant experience to cite
- Pricing structure and payment terms
- Client-specific differentiators for Why Us
- Proposal validity period and signature requirements

## Notes
- Section 4 (Scope) and Section 8 (Assumptions & Exclusions) must stay consistent with each other — don't list something as in-scope and also as excluded.
- Section 9 (Why Us) must be tailored per client; do not reuse generic differentiators verbatim across proposals.
- Follow the firm's style profile (10-Profiles/proposals-style) for voice on top of this structural template.

TSP Proposal Style Profile
## 1. Standard Sections in Their Usual Order

Based on the sample documents, TSP proposals and Statements of Work follow this general sequence:

1. **Executive Summary** – High-level overview of scope, objectives, and value proposition
2. **Methodology / Approach Overview** – Description of the implementation or delivery methodology (e.g., SAP Activate, phased approach)
3. **Project Scope and Deliverables** – Defined list of Services and Deliverables tied to a Statement of Work
4. **Project Plan / Phases and Milestones** – Timeline, key phases, quality gates
5. **Roles and Responsibilities** – RACI matrix or equivalent; expectations for both TSP and client personnel
6. **Resourcing** – Team composition, key personnel commitments
7. **Fee Structure / Commercial Terms** – Project Fee, billing basis (T&M or fixed), exclusions, payment terms
8. **Terms and Conditions** – Legal definitions (Deliverables, Documentation, IP Rights, etc.), MSA references, travel/expense policy

> *Note: Not every section appears in every document type; SOWs emphasize legal/commercial sections while proposal decks emphasize methodology and scope.*

---

## 2. Tone and Language Patterns

| Dimension | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Formality** | Formal to semi-formal; business-professional throughout |
| **Person** | Third person for the firm ("Service Provider will…", "TSP…"); second person occasionally in client-facing narrative ("your projects", "your installed base customers") |
| **Sentence style** | Mix of medium-length declarative sentences in narrative sections and short bullet fragments in lists; dense noun phrases common in legal/definition sections |
| **Voice** | Active voice preferred in methodology sections; passive voice appears in legal/contractual sections ("will be deemed to have originated", "will be tested or otherwise have their quality checked") |
| **Technical register** | SAP product names and acronyms used without explanation (S/4HANA, SAC, RISE, DDA, FPS, ABAP); assumes a technically literate reader |

---

## 3. Recurring Boilerplate and Stock Phrasing

**Definitions sections** follow a consistent pattern of bold-labeled terms followed by expansive legal prose:

> *"'Deliverables' shall mean products, work products, software developed, results or output of Services, final version of items of work and documentation, or any Materials procured or prepared by TSP and delivered to Client in connection with a Statement of Work."*

**Methodology framing** uses a phased, structured voice:

> *"This project will utilize a phased approach."*

> *"We will have a workshop phase, a deep dive phase broken down by functional areas…a realization phase (content build) and a presentation and sign-off phase."*

**Quality and risk language** recurs in project delivery sections:

> *"…ensures no deliverable is overlooked, and ensures [firm] and [client] agree to move forward together during each phase of the project."*

> *"…ensures alignment and transparency and reduces risk during the project."*

**Fee and billing boilerplate:**

> *"The fees billed cannot exceed the Project Fee without executing a change order upon mutual agreement of both parties."*

> *"The Project Fee excludes travel, boarding, lodging, and other travel-related expenses."*

> *"Payments are due in accordance with the terms of our Master Services Agreement, or 45 days, whichever is shorter."*

**Specification and progress language (MSA/SOW):**

> *"Service Provider will: (a) keep [Client] advised of the progress of the Services, (b) permit any [Client] representative to review the results of the Services and the Deliverables from time to time, and (c) keep records of the Services, which [Client] may review from time to time."*

## 4. Formatting Conventions

- **Headings**: Title Case for section headings; sentence case acceptable for sub-items
- **Bullet lists**: Heavily used for scope items, methodology steps, deliverables, and feature descriptions; em-dash (—) or bullet (•) used interchangeably
- **Bold text**: Used to call out defined terms (e.g., **"Deliverables"**, **"Documentation"**), section labels, and key product names
- **Numbered lists**: Used for sequential steps (e.g., project phases, quality gates, agenda items)
- **Tables**: Used for agenda/session breakdowns (presenter, topic, time) and for structured comparison content
- **Figures**: Referenced inline with captions (e.g., *"Figure 7: Activate Brownfield Methodology Overview"*)
- **Confidentiality notice**: Appears in footers on legal documents (e.g., *"CONFIDENTIAL"* with document version number)
- **Document versioning**: Reflected in file names and footers (e.g., *"v2025"*, *"DRAFT v1"*, version numbers such as *"155712444v.4"*)
- **Page references**: Legal/MSA documents include page numbers and section references (e.g., *"Master Services Agreement / Page 3 of 23"*)

## 5. How TSP Positions Its SAP Expertise and Differentiators

Based solely on what appears in the sample:

- **Methodology credibility**: TSP references SAP Activate methodology explicitly as the framework for project delivery, signaling alignment with SAP's own recommended approach for S/4HANA implementations.
- **Structured quality gates**: TSP emphasizes a **Quality Gate Process** as a risk-reduction mechanism—positioned as a differentiator ensuring joint client-TSP sign-off at each phase before proceeding.
- **Breadth across SAP portfolio**: The sample documents reference S/4HANA (on-premise and cloud, public and private editions), SAC (SAP Analytics Cloud), RISE with SAP, SAP Fiori, and SAP BTP—conveying broad SAP platform coverage rather than narrow module expertise.
- **Phased, iterative delivery**: TSP consistently advocates for phased engagements over single large-scope contracts, framing this as lower risk and higher long-term value.
- **Fit-to-Standard orientation**: Proposals reflect a preference for adopting SAP best practices over customizing to legacy processes, positioning this as a strategic recommendation rather than a constraint.
- **Client transparency**: Regular status reporting, shared tooling (e.g., Microsoft SharePoint, PPM100), and RACI matrices are presented as standard practice, reinforcing a collaborative and accountable delivery model.
- **Offshore/blended resourcing**: Where referenced, offshore Center of Excellence resources are framed as a delivery option that supports cost-effective engagement.
